AI STRATEGY NOTES
=================

CURRENT STATE
-------------
- NEW: Score-based AI that compares all actions on unified scale
- Uses sqrt(cost_pct) to balance cheap vs expensive purchases
- Emergency mode for food crisis (precise calculation)
- Begging as fallback when nothing affordable (free +1 coin)
- Beats old priority-based AI 100% of the time (8.97x power ratio)


KEY DECISIONS
-------------
1. Train vs Recruit - which gives more power per coin?
2. When to buy farms/economy vs army?
3. When to save for multiplier upgrades (SL, barracks, plantation)?


POWER/COST CALCULATIONS
-----------------------
train_value = (troops * ppt * 0.01) / train_cost
recruit_value = (rp * ppt) / recruit_cost

But recruit has hidden costs (farm strain)...


FARM STRAIN - 5 VARIABLES
-------------------------
1. SUPER EMERGENCY (0/1)
   - Will recruiting make it impossible to buy enough farms/plantations to survive?
   - If 1 → hard veto, never recruit

2. SUPER EMERGENCY COST (clicks, % coins)
   - How many farm/plantation clicks and what % of coins needed to avoid super emergency?
   - The hidden cost of recruiting

3. EMERGENCY (0/1)
   - Will recruiting force me into emergency territory (must buy farms or starve)?
   - Calculated precisely: days_to_afford_farm > days_of_food
   - NOT arbitrary threshold like "< 30 days"

4. EMERGENCY DISTANCE (number)
   - How many recruit clicks until I hit emergency?
   - Higher = more buffer = safer
   - Could express as fraction: this recruit uses 1/N of my safety margin

5. EMERGENCY SEVERITY (clicks, % coins)
   - If in emergency, how expensive to escape?
   - How many farm/plantation/colony clicks needed?
   - What % of my coins?


COMBINED FARM STRAIN FORMULA
----------------------------
if super_emergency: return INFINITY (veto)

strain = (super_emergency_cost.coins / my_coins) * w1
       + emergency * w2
       + (1 / emergency_distance) * w3
       + (emergency_severity.coins / my_coins) * w4

Weights need tuning via playtesting.


TRUE COST OF RECRUITING
-----------------------
true_recruit_cost = recruit_cost
                  + farms_needed * farm_cost
                  + plantations_needed * plantation_cost
                  + colonies_needed * colony_cost

recruit_adjusted_value = (rp * ppt) / true_recruit_cost
OR
recruit_adjusted_value = recruit_value - farm_strain


DECISION FRAMEWORK
------------------
recruit_adjusted_value vs train_value → pick higher

Also consider:
- Am I close to a multiplier upgrade? (SL, barracks, plantation)
- If yes, save for it (future recruits/farms will be better)


INCOME CALCULATION
------------------
income_per_day = (troops * ppt * 4)      # passive loot
               + (clicks_per_second * 1)  # begging

Used to calculate days_to_afford_X precisely.


RECRUIT TIMING - "WAIT FOR MULTIPLIER?" FACTOR
-----------------------------------------------
Two variables:
1. seconds_until_upgrade - how long until I can afford target upgrade
2. target_upgrade - which upgrade am I saving for (SL, barracks, etc.)

From target_upgrade, derive rp improvement:
- SL: +1 to rp directly
- Barracks: +1 to SL power → more SLs per click → bigger future rp
- Military base: +1 to barracks power → even more downstream

Decision: is the rp improvement worth the wait?
- Short wait + big improvement → delay recruiting
- Long wait + small improvement → recruit now

Lost passive income during wait = opportunity cost.

This is a MINOR factor (recruit is cheap vs upgrades).
Only matters if very close to upgrade (like < 3 seconds).

SEPARATE QUESTION: "Which multiplier upgrade should I target?"
That's the upgrade prioritization problem, not recruit vs train.


ECONOMY CHAIN FORMULA
---------------------
For each economy button (farm, plantation, colony):

  score = button_value / sqrt(cost_pct)

Where:
  button_value = output * building_value
  output = buildings per click (fp, pp, cp)
  cost_pct = button_cost / current_coins

Building values (3x per tier):
  farm = 1
  plantation = 3
  colony = 9

Why sqrt?
- Compresses differences at low cost_pct (1% vs 5% = both cheap)
- Still penalizes high cost_pct (50% = expensive)
- Continuous, no arbitrary thresholds

Example scenario verification:
- First colony at 36% cost → wins vs plantation (should buy)
- Second colony at 55% cost → loses vs plantation (shouldn't buy)
- Farm at 2.5% vs plantation at 13% → farm wins (much cheaper, same output value)


CROSS-TIER PRIORITIZATION (Army vs Economy)
-------------------------------------------
When to focus on army chain vs economy chain?

Two factors:

1. POWER CLOSENESS - how close is the battle?

   closeness = min(my_power, enemy_power) / max(my_power, enemy_power)

   closeness ≈ 1.0 (tied): army investment HIGH IMPACT (could swing battle)
   closeness ≈ 0.1 (far apart): army investment LOW IMPACT (outcome decided)

   When far apart (either ahead or behind), army doesn't matter much.
   Might as well invest in economy for compound growth.

2. PROJECTED FOOD STRAIN - how constrained is future growth?

   capacity_used = current_troops / max_sustainable_troops
   recruits_until_full = (max_sustainable - current_troops) / rp

   If lots of headroom: army focus ok
   If near capacity: need economy to unlock more growth

   This is DIFFERENT from immediate food strain (will I starve?).
   This is about: "am I on a path toward hitting a ceiling?"

Combining them:

   Base building values:
   economy: farm=1, plantation=3, colony=9
   army: SL=2, barracks=6, MB=18, kingdom=54 (2x economy equivalent)

   Army has 2x base bias because troops generate loot → compounds more
   (loot buys both army AND economy)

   Then apply bonuses:
   army_score = base_score * (1 + closeness * k1)
   economy_score = base_score * (1 + strain * k2)

   Results:
   - Neutral (low closeness, low strain) → army wins (2x base)
   - High strain → economy bonus overcomes 2x bias
   - High closeness → army wins even more
   - High closeness + high strain → depends on k1 vs k2 weights


TRAIN/RECRUIT VS ARMY CHAIN
---------------------------
Train and recruit get ~1.2x bonus because:
- They're immediate (no waiting for payoff)
- They don't inflate costs of other things (own scaling pools)

Army chain items share cost pool - buying SL makes barracks more expensive.


FULL SCORING SYSTEM
-------------------
For each possible action, calculate score:

  score = (output * building_value * modifiers) / sqrt(cost_pct)

Building values:
  Economy: farm=1, plantation=3, colony=9
  Army: SL=2, barracks=6, MB=18, kingdom=54 (2x economy)
  Train: troops * ppt * 0.01 (immediate power gain)
  Recruit: rp * ppt (immediate power gain)

Modifiers:
  Train/recruit: * 1.2 (no-inflation bonus)
  Army chain: * (1 + closeness * k1)
  Economy chain: * (1 + strain * k2)
  Recruit: - farm_strain_penalty

Where:
  closeness = min(my_power, enemy_power) / max(my_power, enemy_power)
  strain = current_troops / max_sustainable_troops
  cost_pct = cost / current_coins

Pick action with highest score. If nothing affordable, beg.


SIMULATION RESULTS (2024)
-------------------------
Tested new score-based AI vs old priority-based AI.
50 simulations, 200 days.

Optimized parameters:
  K_CLOSENESS = 0.5      (army bonus when battle is close)
  K_STRAIN = 2.0         (economy bonus when food constrained)
  NO_INFLATE_BONUS = 1.2 (train/recruit bonus)
  Army multiplier = 10x  (SL=10, barracks=100, MB=1000, kingdom=10000)

WHY 10x INSTEAD OF 3x?
Human playtesting revealed AI was recruiting too early instead of
building multiplier chain. With 3x values:
- Recruit at 1% cost: score = 1/sqrt(0.01) = 10
- Barracks at 50% cost: score = 9/sqrt(0.5) = 12.7 (barely higher!)

The 3x ratio doesn't capture the TRUE compound value of multipliers.
Each barracks gives +1 SL per click, and each SL gives +1 rp, so
barracks value compounds over time.

With 10x values, barracks (100) clearly beats recruit (1), making
the AI prioritize the multiplier chain like human players do.

Simulation tested 10x vs 3x: 10x wins 6x more power at 20 cps.

Results at actual game click rates:
  4 cps:  100% win rate, 6.79x ratio
  8 cps:  100% win rate, 18.91x ratio
  25 cps: 100% win rate, 38.38x ratio

Note: maxIterations = clicks (not clicks*10 - that was a bug)

The game is fully deterministic - no randomness.
New vs New always ties, solo runs always reach same power.


EMERGENCY MODE FIX
------------------
Bug found: when coins >= farmCost, daysToAffordFarm = 0, so
"0 > daysOfFood" was always false, even at 0 food.

Fix: emergency = (daysToAfford > daysOfFood || daysOfFood <= 0) && netConsume > 0

This ensures we buy farms when:
1. We can't afford farm before starving, OR
2. We're already at 0 food and still need farms
